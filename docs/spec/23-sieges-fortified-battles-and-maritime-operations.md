# 23. Sieges, fortified battles and maritime operations

## 23.1 A fortress is a place, not a progress bar

Castles and walled towns occupy actual terrain with walls, towers, gates, ditches, roads, stores, water, surrounding settlements and possible relief routes. Their defensive strength comes from geography, construction, garrison, supplies, preparation and attackers' choices. A castle cannot receive a free generic fortification score that replaces its physical situation.

## 23.2 Siege begins on the campaign map

A besieging force must arrive, secure access, establish camps and attempt to isolate the defender. Supply lines, local food, roads, winter conditions, water and siege equipment constrain it. The defender's stores, water sources, possible foraging routes, nearby allies and hidden exits matter. A surrounding region may remain contested; siege is not automatic ownership.

Blockade effectiveness depends on available manpower and terrain access. A fortress with access to a protected river landing can remain supplied until the attacker controls that landing or interrupts shipping.

## 23.3 Siege options

Besiege/starve, negotiate surrender, blockade, raid surrounding land, build works, sap/undermine where terrain and tools permit, bombard with era-appropriate artillery, storm walls or attempt a relief sortie. Assault generates a real-time tactical battle incorporating persistent buildings and siege works. Defender sorties and attacking relief armies can create field battles around the same fortification landscape.

## 23.4 Siege state and damage

Wall integrity, gates, breaches, towers, siege works, fires, stored provisions, equipment, manpower and civilian condition persist. Repairs consume labor/materials. Damaged fortifications remain damaged after battle until fixed. Collapsing walls create tactical obstacles and reachable breaches consistent with campaign state. Civilian casualties and destruction affect urban economy and legitimacy.

## 23.5 Assault gameplay

Allow ladders, rams, movable mantlets, siege towers and pre-gunpowder engines where technology/resources permit; each requires manufacture, transport, assembly and terrain-appropriate placement. Siege objectives include gates, wall sectors, streets, strongpoints and surrender. Units navigate narrow passages with serious congestion and morale challenges. Walls aren't merely high-defense ordinary field tiles.

## 23.6 Water and ships

Maritime trade, fishing, fleets, ferries, naval patrols, coastal blockades and troop transport exist at the **campaign level**. Ships have capacity, crew, weather exposure, ports and route requirements. Maritime access can enable oversea commerce, strategic troop movement and supply. Sea conditions affect departure, movement and losses. Landing troops requires a feasible beach/harbor and time, and an opposed landing can trigger a coastal land battle.

**Committed scope boundary:** dedicated real-time ship-to-ship naval battles are **excluded** from the principal tactical game. Sea engagements resolve through the campaign naval model using vessel capabilities, positioning, sea state and command, with meaningful outcomes and losses. Never substitute arbitrary “fleet power vs fleet power” rolls; use the same causality and quantity conservation standards at aggregated resolution. This preserves focus on large land battles rather than a second enormous naval combat game.

## 23.7 River naval activity

Navigable rivers may carry barges, ferries and small military craft subject to draught, water level and bridges. River crossings in land battles have actual width/depth/current and can be defended, blocked or modified under engineering constraints. No unlimited boat spawning on rivers.

## 23.8 Weather, terrain and water in siege warfare

Fortress site elevation, slopes, earthworks, floodplain position and connected roads belong to world geography. Water supply may derive from actual springs, wells, rain stores, cisterns, streams or aquifers as supported by terrain/geology. Rainfall and snow affect water storage, camp conditions, construction and siege machinery. Floods can block approaches, damage temporary bridges or create disease/shortage pressure through real conditions; winter can close logistics while also limiting outside water and fodder.

Siege assaults load the same terrain, wind, precipitation, ground and water data as other tactical engagements. River interdiction uses actual navigable depth and supply routes. A siege cannot magically drain a river or force defenders to starve without exhaustion of actual connected stocks and access.
