# 16. Government policy, administration and domestic politics

## 16.1 The player's decision scale

Kingdom policies establish priorities and institutions, not precise household instructions. Region directives enable autonomous managers. Local interventions affect strategic sites. The interface must support *delegation by default* and exception-based interaction. Player decisions should be legible in terms of money, material access, time, political consequences and opportunity cost.

## 16.2 Available policy areas

- Fiscal policy: ordinary and extraordinary taxes, spending priorities, loan/revenue decisions, exemptions.
- Land and production: grants, access rights, settlement charters, crop incentives, forestry rules, mines and economic development.
- Trade: tolls, routes, merchants, embargoes, customs and port access.
- Social/legal: local rights, noble and church privileges, religious toleration/persecution within setting rules, peasant obligations, justice and administration.
- Military: recruitment obligations, garrison policy, supply organization, army pay, fortification programs, training and command institutions.
- Infrastructure: roads, bridges, depots, harbors, mills, defensive lines.

All policies interact with existing authority; a decree that cannot be enforced should be marked limited or resisted, not applied uniformly for free.

## 16.3 Stability and legitimacy

Do not maintain a magical national “happiness” variable as primary causal state. Track specific economic pressures, political disputes, legitimacy, local security, cultural/religious tensions, tax grievances, ruler reputation and administrative confidence. UI may summarize these as stability/risk indexes, derived from contributing factors with drill-down reasons.

## 16.4 Institutions and command capacity

As kingdoms grow, administrative range, communication and bureaucracy become limiting factors. The crown can establish provincial administrations, roads, courts and regular accounting, but requires skilled staff, income, political consent and time. A politically decentralized empire may command a formidable total population yet mobilize only a fraction reliably.

## 16.5 Personalities, not character micromanagement

Important rulers, lords and governors have goals, relationships, competence and loyalty. They may propose agreements, resist extraordinary taxes or prioritize their estates. Player uses diplomacy, appointments, concessions, reforms and occasional political interventions. There is no mandatory romance, genealogy minigame or detailed household character scheduling.
