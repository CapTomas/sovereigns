# 24. Resolution, aftermath, occupation and persistent consequence

## 24.1 Manual vs automatic combat

The player can fight battles manually or choose autoresolution. Both use the **same authoritative unit definitions, actual condition, equipment, troop experience, terrain, weather, defenses, supplies, commanders and operational context**. Autoresolution may be a calibrated lower-resolution model; results need not match manual play exactly, but must not systematically reverse environmental advantages or offer magic losses.

Provide an estimate with uncertainty and a clear explanation of primary factors. Do not reveal hidden enemy details absent intelligence. Retreat and surrender outcomes must be possible in autoresolution, not just win/lose extermination.

## 24.2 Battle time and state commits

The battle consumes actual elapsed world time, ammunition and provisions. Units finish at real positions, morale/fatigue states and survivable manpower. Survivors, wounded, routed stragglers, prisoners, transport animals and carried equipment update campaign entities. No instant fully replenished army after returning from tactical mode.

Terrain/structure changes commit to the persistent world: burned farms, broken bridges, cut trees if materially relevant, destroyed gates, damaged roads, fieldworks, altered channel crossings and settlement damage. Cosmetic footprints/tracks may fade and need not be stored forever. State updates must be attributable and consistent across save/reload.

## 24.3 Occupation is a process

Winning a field engagement grants local military advantage, not automatic legal sovereignty or instant taxation. Occupation requires holding key sites, routes and administrative centers and may be contested by guerilla/irregular action and surviving local authorities. Taking a fortress may secure a pass; conquering a whole kingdom requires more than defeating one army. Civilian economy adapts to control, security and negotiated agreements.

## 24.4 Prisoners, spoils and recovery

Prisoners require guarding, food and political decisions (ransom, exchange, release, detention); equipment may be recovered depending on control and time. Wounded can recover or die based on care, infection risk, movement and supplies. Routed regiments may regroup with remaining soldiers; destroyed experience and lost gear have lasting economic effects. Pursuit and battlefield possession change how much material is recovered.

## 24.5 Destruction and the economic network

If a bridge is destroyed, mark the transport link unusable or reduced and recalculate flows; shipments already en route reroute, delay or become stranded. If fields burn, corresponding upcoming harvest capacity is reduced. If a settlement is occupied, local taxation and access change according to rights and military presence. No duplicate losses or double-applied battle damage.

## 24.6 Historical memory and revisiting places

Major battles enter the chronicle with actual date, location, participants and outcome. Battle sites may have persistent ruins, memorials, altered land or changed importance, but optional commemorative visuals do not rewrite physics. A later battle in the same valley uses familiar geography plus justified intervening changes. This historical continuity is one of Sovereigns' signature experiences.
