# 18. Knowledge, military traditions and social change

## 18.1 Progress without a magical tech tree

The game contains a readable **knowledge and institutions overview**, but no instantaneous purchase of civilization-wide breakthroughs using abstract science points. Learning originates in trained workers, military experience, scholars, religious or civic institutions, artisans, craftsmen, contact and accumulated investment. Discovery and adoption are separate: a method might be known but not yet used in isolated regions lacking tools or capital.

## 18.2 Domains of knowledge

Agriculture, construction, metalworking, transport, navigation, administration, medicine/sanitation and military organization. Regions may lead in particular domains due to local need, institutions and trade. Military doctrines change through practice, commanders and equipment; learning may travel with refugees or mercenaries.

## 18.3 Era bounds

The design supports an extended medieval-like arc without industrialization. Progress creates meaningful but bounded improvement in tools, structures, fortification forms, shipbuilding and arms. No arbitrary rapid technological leaps, magic devices or firearm-dominated endgame. Distinct styles can coexist across regions rather than all factions synchronizing to a global technology age.

## 18.4 Player decisions

Fund training halls, workshops, court scholars, guilds, monasteries, smithing contracts or foreign knowledge exchanges. Standardize equipment and military drilling. Improve literacy/administration where institutions permit. Benefits are gradual, costful and grounded in capacity; they should make strategy more varied rather than guarantee one optimal linear research path.
